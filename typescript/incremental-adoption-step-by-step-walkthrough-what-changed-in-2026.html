Version pinning deserves a special mention. Lock the toolchain version in the repository so every developer and every CI agent sees identical behavior, and upgrade on your schedule — not when a random warning forces your hand.

  • Install or update to the latest stable TypeScript release before touching configuration

incremental adoption: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most incremental adoption problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately

Conclusion

incremental adoption rewards a small, consistent investment far more than a heroic one-off effort. Start with the checklist above, keep the setup boring and documented, and revisit it once a quarter. The rest of your workflow gets quietly easier from that.

FAQ

What is the biggest mistake beginners make with incremental adoption?

Skipping the fundamentals and jumping straight to advanced configuration. Understand the default behavior first; most “problems” with incremental adoption are actually undocumented expectations colliding with sensible defaults.

unknown or any — which should I use?

Prefer unknown for values whose shape you do not control: it forces narrowing before use. any disables checking entirely and should be a deliberate, commented escape hatch.

The Case for Roslyn on macOS in 2023

Roslyn on macOS: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most Roslyn on macOS problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Treating local and CI environments as different worlds; the same commands should run in both
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make Roslyn on macOS a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

FAQ

Can I use Roslyn on macOS in an existing project, or only in new ones?

Both. Retrofitting Roslyn on macOS into an existing project is common — introduce it incrementally, module by module, rather than in one sweeping change. New projects should simply start with it enabled from day one.

How do I keep my Roslyn on macOS setup up to date?

Subscribe to the official release notes, upgrade on a schedule (quarterly works well for most teams), and keep a changelog of your own configuration decisions so upgrades stay boring and reversible.

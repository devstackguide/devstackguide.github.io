Common Mistakes with F# on .NET 10 (and Fixes)

Most F# on .NET 10 problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them

Conclusion

None of the individual steps here are difficult — the value comes from doing them consistently across the team. Pick two improvements from this guide, apply them this week, and schedule the rest. Momentum beats perfection with F# on .NET 10.

FAQ

How do I keep my F# on .NET 10 setup up to date?

Subscribe to the official release notes, upgrade on a schedule (quarterly works well for most teams), and keep a changelog of your own configuration decisions so upgrades stay boring and reversible.

What is the biggest mistake beginners make with F# on .NET 10?

Skipping the fundamentals and jumping straight to advanced configuration. Understand the default behavior first; most “problems” with F# on .NET 10 are actually undocumented expectations colliding with sensible defaults.

Why F# and native AOT Matters in 2023

The reason F# and native AOT keeps coming up in 2023 is simple: projects grow, teams grow, and the tooling has to keep up. Getting this part right early prevents the kind of slow decay that turns a clean codebase into a maze of workarounds within a year.

Setting Up F# and native AOT the Right Way

Before changing anything, take five minutes to write down what bothers you about the current setup. Is it build speed? Debugging? Deployment? Targeted fixes to F# and native AOT stick better than wholesale rewrites driven by curiosity.

  • Set up a minimal CI job that runs the same commands developers run locally
  • Wire the basic commands into your editor so everyday tasks are one keystroke away
  • Keep all F# and native AOT-related files in source control, including local overrides you choose to ignore deliberately

Common Mistakes with F# and native AOT (and Fixes)

Most F# and native AOT problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them
  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead

Conclusion

None of the individual steps here are difficult — the value comes from doing them consistently across the team. Pick two improvements from this guide, apply them this week, and schedule the rest. Momentum beats perfection with F# and native AOT.

Why F# and ASP.NET Core Deserves Your Attention

Setting Up F# and ASP.NET Core the Right Way

The workflow itself is straightforward once the pieces are in place. Most of the value comes from the first hour of setup, after which the tooling quietly does its job in the background while you focus on actual product work.

  • Set up a minimal CI job that runs the same commands developers run locally
  • Wire the basic commands into your editor so everyday tasks are one keystroke away
  • Install or update to the latest stable F# release before touching configuration

F# and ASP.NET Core: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most F# and ASP.NET Core problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately
  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors
  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make F# and ASP.NET Core a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

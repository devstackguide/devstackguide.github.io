property based testing: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most property based testing problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately
  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once
  • Treating local and CI environments as different worlds; the same commands should run in both

Conclusion

Tooling should serve the code, not the other way around. If your property based testing setup makes simple tasks harder, simplify it ruthlessly. The best configuration is the one nobody on the team has to think about.

FAQ

Is property based testing suitable for large teams?

It scales well precisely because it standardizes the everyday workflow. The larger the team, the more value you get from shared configuration, enforced conventions, and a single documented setup path.

Is F# a good first language?

For learners who like structured thinking, yes — its emphasis on modeling data precisely and avoiding null teaches excellent habits. Most developers, however, arrive at F# after some C# or another mainstream language.

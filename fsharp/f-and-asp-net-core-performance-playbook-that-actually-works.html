F# and ASP.NET Core: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most F# and ASP.NET Core problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once
  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make F# and ASP.NET Core a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

FAQ

Can F# and C# live in the same solution?

Absolutely — they compile to the same IL and interoperate in both directions, with small conventions to respect (F# prefers interfaces and options; C# sees them as ordinary .NET types).

Can I use F# and ASP.NET Core in an existing project, or only in new ones?

Both. Retrofitting F# and ASP.NET Core into an existing project is common — introduce it incrementally, module by module, rather than in one sweeping change. New projects should simply start with it enabled from day one.

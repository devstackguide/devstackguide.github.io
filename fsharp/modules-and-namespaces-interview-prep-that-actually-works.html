modules and namespaces: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most modules and namespaces problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately
  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make modules and namespaces a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

FAQ

Can F# and C# live in the same solution?

Absolutely — they compile to the same IL and interoperate in both directions, with small conventions to respect (F# prefers interfaces and options; C# sees them as ordinary .NET types).

Can I use modules and namespaces in an existing project, or only in new ones?

Both. Retrofitting modules and namespaces into an existing project is common — introduce it incrementally, module by module, rather than in one sweeping change. New projects should simply start with it enabled from day one.

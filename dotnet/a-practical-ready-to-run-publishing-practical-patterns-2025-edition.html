Why ready-to-run publishing Matters in 2025

The reason ready-to-run publishing keeps coming up in 2025 is simple: projects grow, teams grow, and the tooling has to keep up. Getting this part right early prevents the kind of slow decay that turns a clean codebase into a maze of workarounds within a year.

Getting Started with ready-to-run publishing

The workflow itself is straightforward once the pieces are in place. Most of the value comes from the first hour of setup, after which the tooling quietly does its job in the background while you focus on actual product work.

  • Set up a minimal CI job that runs the same commands developers run locally
  • Turn on caching where the tool supports it; the payoff grows with project size
  • Enable the strictness or validation options early — retrofitting them later is far more painful

ready-to-run publishing: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most ready-to-run publishing problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately
  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors
  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make ready-to-run publishing a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

FAQ

Should I upgrade from .NET 8 to .NET 10 now?

For most apps, yes — the upgrade path is well documented and tooling-assisted. Note that .NET 8 support ends in November 2026, so planning the move to .NET 10 avoids a rushed migration.

Can I use ready-to-run publishing in an existing project, or only in new ones?

Both. Retrofitting ready-to-run publishing into an existing project is common — introduce it incrementally, module by module, rather than in one sweeping change. New projects should simply start with it enabled from day one.

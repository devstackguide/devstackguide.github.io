The Case for testing with xUnit in 2023

Finally, testing with xUnit has matured noticeably by 2023. Features that used to require clever hacks are now first-class, which means older advice online is often outdated. That is exactly why a current, opinionated walkthrough is worth your time.

Common Mistakes with testing with xUnit (and Fixes)

Most testing with xUnit problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately

Conclusion

None of the individual steps here are difficult — the value comes from doing them consistently across the team. Pick two improvements from this guide, apply them this week, and schedule the rest. Momentum beats perfection with testing with xUnit.

FAQ

How do I keep my testing with xUnit setup up to date?

Subscribe to the official release notes, upgrade on a schedule (quarterly works well for most teams), and keep a changelog of your own configuration decisions so upgrades stay boring and reversible.

Should I upgrade from .NET 8 to .NET 10 now?

For most apps, yes — the upgrade path is well documented and tooling-assisted. Note that .NET 8 support ends in November 2026, so planning the move to .NET 10 avoids a rushed migration.

Common Mistakes with logging best practices (and Fixes)

Every experienced developer has lost an afternoon to at least one of the issues below. The pattern behind all of them: a default left unexamined, or an assumption imported from a different ecosystem where the rules are subtly different.

  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors
  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once
  • Treating local and CI environments as different worlds; the same commands should run in both

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make logging best practices a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

FAQ

Where can I find reliable documentation for logging best practices?

Start with the official documentation, then curated community guides. Be skeptical of tutorials older than two years — the 2023 landscape looks noticeably different from even the 2020 advice still ranking in search results.

What is the biggest mistake beginners make with logging best practices?

Skipping the fundamentals and jumping straight to advanced configuration. Understand the default behavior first; most “problems” with logging best practices are actually undocumented expectations colliding with sensible defaults.

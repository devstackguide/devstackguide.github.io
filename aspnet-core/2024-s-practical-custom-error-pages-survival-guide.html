The Case for custom error pages in 2024

It helps to think of custom error pages as infrastructure rather than a one-time setup decision. Standards drift, dependencies update, and team members rotate. Investing a small amount of attention here pays compound interest across every project that follows.

custom error pages Setup, Step by Step

Start small and grow the setup as the project demands it. The most common failure mode is importing a giant configuration from the internet, half of which nobody on the team understands. Every setting below exists for a reason — adopt it deliberately.

  • Turn on caching where the tool supports it; the payoff grows with project size
  • Install or update to the latest stable ASP.NET Core release before touching configuration
  • Schedule a quarterly review to prune settings that no longer earn their place

Common Mistakes with custom error pages (and Fixes)

Most custom error pages problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Copy-pasting configuration from an old project without understanding what each block does — audit every imported setting once
  • Treating local and CI environments as different worlds; the same commands should run in both
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make custom error pages a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

FAQ

How do I keep my custom error pages setup up to date?

Subscribe to the official release notes, upgrade on a schedule (quarterly works well for most teams), and keep a changelog of your own configuration decisions so upgrades stay boring and reversible.

Minimal APIs or controllers — which should I choose?

Minimal APIs shine for small focused services and microservices; controllers remain comfortable for large apps with lots of shared conventions, filters, and attribute-based behavior. Both are first-class and fully supported.

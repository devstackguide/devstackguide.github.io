The Case for background services in 2025

It helps to think of background services as infrastructure rather than a one-time setup decision. Standards drift, dependencies update, and team members rotate. Investing a small amount of attention here pays compound interest across every project that.

Getting Started with background services

The workflow itself is straightforward once the pieces are in place. Most of the value comes from the first hour of setup, after which the tooling quietly does its job in the background while you focus on actual product work.

  • Turn on caching where the tool supports it; the payoff grows with project size
  • Enable the strictness or validation options early — retrofitting them later is far more painful
  • Schedule a quarterly review to prune settings that no longer earn their place

Common Mistakes with background services (and Fixes)

Every experienced developer has lost an afternoon to at least one of the issues below. The pattern behind all of them: a default left unexamined, or an assumption imported from a different ecosystem where the rules are subtly different.

  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Treating local and CI environments as different worlds; the same commands should run in both
  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors

Conclusion

The takeaway is simple: make background services a deliberate choice rather than an accident of history. Document what you adopt, automate what you can, and prune what stops earning its place. That discipline compounds across every project you touch.

FAQ

Minimal APIs or controllers — which should I choose?

Minimal APIs shine for small focused services and microservices; controllers remain comfortable for large apps with lots of shared conventions, filters, and attribute-based behavior. Both are first-class and fully supported.

Can I use background services in an existing project, or only in new ones?

Both. Retrofitting background services into an existing project is common — introduce it incrementally, module by module, rather than in one sweeping change. New projects should simply start with it enabled from day one.

The Case for IIS hosting models in 2024

A surprising amount of developer frustration traces back to IIS hosting models. Bugs that appear only on one machine, builds that pass locally and fail in CI, onboarding that takes days instead of hours — all of these usually point at the same root cause.

IIS hosting models: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most IIS hosting models problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately
  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors

Conclusion

Tooling should serve the code, not the other way around. If your IIS hosting models setup makes simple tasks harder, simplify it ruthlessly. The best configuration is the one nobody on the team has to think about.

Is IIS hosting models still relevant in 2024?

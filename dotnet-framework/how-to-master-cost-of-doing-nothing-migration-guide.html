The Case for cost of doing nothing in 2023

The reason cost of doing nothing keeps coming up in 2023 is simple: projects grow, teams grow, and the tooling has to keep up. Getting this part right early prevents the kind of slow decay that turns a clean codebase into a maze of workarounds within a year.

cost of doing nothing Setup, Step by Step

The workflow itself is straightforward once the pieces are in place. Most of the value comes from the first hour of setup, after which the tooling quietly does its job in the background while you focus on actual product work.

  • Set up a minimal CI job that runs the same commands developers run locally
  • Add a short README section so a new teammate can get a working environment in under 30 minutes
  • Schedule a quarterly review to prune settings that no longer earn their place

cost of doing nothing: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most cost of doing nothing problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead

Conclusion

Tooling should serve the code, not the other way around. If your cost of doing nothing setup makes simple tasks harder, simplify it ruthlessly. The best configuration is the one nobody on the team has to think about.

FAQ

Does cost of doing nothing work on Linux, macOS, and Windows?

Yes. Cross-platform support is a core expectation in 2023, and the workflow is nearly identical across operating systems. The few differences that remain are documented in the official setup guides.

Can I run .NET Framework and modern .NET on the same machine?

Yes — they install side by side without conflicts. Many applications deliberately migrate piece by piece while both runtimes coexist on the same servers.

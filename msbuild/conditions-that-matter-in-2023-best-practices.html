Version pinning deserves a special mention. Lock the toolchain version in the repository so every developer and every CI agent sees identical behavior, and upgrade on your schedule — not when a random warning forces your hand.

  • Wire the basic commands into your editor so everyday tasks are one keystroke away

conditions that matter: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most conditions that matter problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Treating local and CI environments as different worlds; the same commands should run in both
  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead

Conclusion

Tooling should serve the code, not the other way around. If your conditions that matter setup makes simple tasks harder, simplify it ruthlessly. The best configuration is the one nobody on the team has to think about.

FAQ

How long does it take to get comfortable with conditions that matter?

Most developers feel productive within a few days of focused practice and solid within a month of real project work. The concepts click quickly; the edge cases are what experience fills in over time.

How do I find out why a property has its value?

Run msbuild /pp:preprocessed.xml to see all imports flattened, or capture a binary log with -bl and open it in the MSBuild Structured Log Viewer to trace each property's origin.

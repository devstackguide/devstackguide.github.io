The Case for implicit imports in 2024

The reason implicit imports keeps coming up in 2024 is simple: projects grow, teams grow, and the tooling has to keep up. Getting this part right early prevents the kind of slow decay that turns a clean codebase into a maze of workarounds within a year.

Getting Started with implicit imports

Version pinning deserves a special mention. Lock the toolchain version in the repository so every developer and every CI agent sees identical behavior, and upgrade on your schedule — not when a random warning forces your hand.

  • Add a short README section so a new teammate can get a working environment in under 30 minutes
  • Enable the strictness or validation options early — retrofitting them later is far more painful
  • Keep all implicit imports-related files in source control, including local overrides you choose to ignore deliberately

Common Mistakes with implicit imports (and Fixes)

Every experienced developer has lost an afternoon to at least one of the issues below. The pattern behind all of them: a default left unexamined, or an assumption imported from a different ecosystem where the rules are subtly different.

  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Skipping the clean/rebuild check before assuming a bug is real — stale artifacts explain a remarkable share of “impossible” errors

Conclusion

None of the individual steps here are difficult — the value comes from doing them consistently across the team. Pick two improvements from this guide, apply them this week, and schedule the rest. Momentum beats perfection with implicit imports.

FAQ

Is implicit imports still relevant in 2024?

Yes — more than ever. The ecosystem around implicit imports keeps growing, and the skills transfer directly between projects. What changes year to year is mostly the tooling surface, not the underlying concepts, so time spent.

What is the difference between a property and an item?

Properties are single name-value strings used for configuration; items are typed lists (Compile, PackageReference, Content) that carry per-entry metadata and accumulate through Include/Remove/Update.

The Case for content items and globs in 2025

A surprising amount of developer frustration traces back to content items and globs. Bugs that appear only on one machine, builds that pass locally and fail in CI, onboarding that takes days instead of hours — all of these usually point at the same root cause.

Getting Started with content items and globs

Before changing anything, take five minutes to write down what bothers you about the current setup. Is it build speed? Debugging? Deployment? Targeted fixes to content items and globs stick better than wholesale rewrites driven by curiosity.

  • Turn on caching where the tool supports it; the payoff grows with project size
  • Install or update to the latest stable MSBuild release before touching configuration
  • Enable the strictness or validation options early — retrofitting them later is far more painful

content items and globs: Frequent Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

Most content items and globs problems are not exotic — they are the same handful of mistakes repeated across teams. Catching these early is the cheapest quality improvement available, and none of the fixes require deep expertise.

  • Mixing tool versions across the team; pin versions in the repo and let the toolchain enforce them
  • Hand-editing generated files; find the source of truth and change it there instead
  • Ignoring warnings until the output is a wall of noise — fix, suppress with a comment, or configure thresholds deliberately

Conclusion

None of the individual steps here are difficult — the value comes from doing them consistently across the team. Pick two improvements from this guide, apply them this week, and schedule the rest. Momentum beats perfection with content items and globs.

FAQ

How do I find out why a property has its value?

Run msbuild /pp:preprocessed.xml to see all imports flattened, or capture a binary log with -bl and open it in the MSBuild Structured Log Viewer to trace each property's origin.

Is content items and globs still relevant in 2025?

Yes — more than ever. The ecosystem around content items and globs keeps growing, and the skills transfer directly between projects. What changes year to year is mostly the tooling surface, not the underlying concepts, so time.
